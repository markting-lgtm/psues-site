Bali Property Investment: Off-Plan Villas Explained

Buying a villa off-plan is one of the most common ways to approach Bali property investment. You commit to a villa while it is being built, based on the plans and the design, usually at a price set below the price after completion. This guide explains how an off-plan villa in Bali works, how to think about living in it or renting it out, how villa rental management works, and what to check before you buy any Bali investment property.

This is general information, not legal or financial advice. Always use an independent notary and lawyer in Bali, and get your own advice before you invest.

What off-plan means

Off-plan means you buy during construction instead of walking through a finished house. You decide based on the plans, the design, the floor plan and a material schedule that lists what will be built and with which finishes.

The trade-off is time and trust. You wait for completion, and you rely on the developer to build what was promised. That is why the written agreement matters more than any brochure.

Off-plan price vs completion price

The main reason buyers choose off-plan is price. At Psusennes Villas, our three villas of one design in Munggu, the two prices are written down:

  • Price after completion, from February 2027: IDR 6,200,000,000 per villa.

The difference between the two list prices is IDR 500,000,000. In exchange, an off-plan buyer waits for completion and follows the build. Whatever price you agree, make sure it is fixed in the agreement, together with the payment schedule.

Live in it or rent it out?

Decide early how you will use the villa, because it changes what you should look for and how you plan.

Living in it

If the villa will be your home or a long-stay base, focus on daily life: the layout, light and privacy, the neighbourhood, and how far you are from the places you go every week. Our villas are in Munggu, in the Seseh area, about 10 minutes from Seseh Beach. Read our Seseh Beach and Munggu area guide to see if the area suits you.

Renting it out

If you plan to rent the villa out, choose between holiday guests and long-term tenants. The work is very different: holiday rentals need bookings, guest care and housekeeping between stays, while long-term rentals need fewer changes but a careful tenant agreement. Check the zoning and the permits for the kind of rental you have in mind, and ask your notary what applies.

Many owners do both: they use the villa for part of the year and rent it out the rest of the time. Whichever you choose, base your plans on your own research and advice, not on promised figures.

How villa rental management works

If you buy a villa and do not live in it full time, someone has to look after the villa and the guests. Villa rental management usually covers four things:

  1. Listing and bookings: presenting the villa online and handling reservations.
  2. Guests: check-in, questions during the stay, and check-out.
  3. Housekeeping: cleaning, linen, and getting the villa ready for the next stay.
  4. Maintenance: the pool, the garden, and small repairs before they become big ones.

You should also get clear reporting, so you can see bookings and costs from wherever you are. Before you choose a manager, ask how they price their service, how they handle reviews and problems, and how often they report to you.

What to check before you buy

  • The land: your notary should check the land certificate, the owner, the zoning and the building permit (PBG).
  • The lease terms: the start date, the length and the renewal terms, in writing. When does the lease start: at signing or at handover?
  • The payment schedule: for an off-plan villa, payments should be linked to construction stages and written in the agreement.
  • The completion date: and what happens if the timeline moves.
  • Progress updates: how you will see the build during construction, especially if you live abroad.
  • The handover: what you receive, checked against the plans and the material schedule before you take the keys.
  • Rental permission: whether the zoning and permits allow the kind of rental you plan.
  • All taxes and fees: ask your notary to list everything on top of the price.
  • Your own notary and lawyer: do not rely only on the seller's notary.
